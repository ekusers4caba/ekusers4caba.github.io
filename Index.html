Project Wallace, in-development gameplay

The Graveyard Shift (originally Project Afterlife) is a silly, experimental physics-based game made in Unity 6 for the 48-hour UWS GameJam 2026 (with Heather Brander).

You play as the Grim Reaper's unpaid intern. In each level you take control of people who are about to die and must invent goofy, strange ways to finish them off.

Levels feature unique humorous challenges and light dark humour set in 3D environments that lean into Unity 6's physics for a deliberately zany experience.

View Project

The Hill Game (also known as Project Boulder) is a university project created by Heather Brander and the author for their 2nd-year Game Engine Module. Inspired by old-school Halo custom lobbies, the goal is to climb a hill while dodging incoming boulders.

Reaching the peak awards points that are multiplied by how quickly the player gets there. Players start with three lives; each hit costs one life, and running out ends the game.

Developed over 12 weeks under time pressure, it earned a pass grade.

A few known bugs (such as floating boulders after the player is teleported back down) are intentionally left in as a reminder of how much was achieved in a short development cycle.

View Project

Locked Undead (originally Project Warehouse) is a level-design showcase created for the Level Design module of the Video Games Development course at UWS.

Made with Heather Brander and Stefan Dianocu, it served as an introduction to Unity and different approaches to level design.

The project features an aesthetic environment with light interactions (animated main menu, player movement, and a toggleable light switch) that let players explore and immerse themselves.

All assets came from the Unity Asset Store, with audio sourced from YouTube and other online sites. It was built purely as a demonstration of level-design skills rather than a fully functional game.
